# MVP03 | HD2D Church Courtyard

Open `Scenes/MVP03_StainedGlassChapel.unity` and press Play. Move the light-clothed pixel character with WASD or the arrow keys. Movement is limited to four directions; when two axes are held, the most recently pressed axis takes priority. Opposing keys on one axis cancel. The camera now faces straight along +Z, with **zero yaw and roll and an 18-degree downward pitch**, keeping the frontal framing with a higher view of the ground. W/S move along Z and A/D along X. The camera stays aligned with the character's X coordinate and keeps their torso at screen centre, including when climbing stairs. **Q moves closer, E moves farther, and the mouse wheel zooms smoothly between 5 and 15 metres**. The dark-clothed companion stays in the courtyard and faces the camera.

Press **Space** to fire a 3D light bolt in the last movement direction (camera-forward before moving). Each press fires once, with a 0.35-second cooldown. The bolt has a solid emissive sphere, a Fresnel shell, rotating mesh rings, a short ribbon and mesh sparks. Its cyan point light illuminates nearby stone. Swept sphere collision triggers a surface ripple, an expanding flash and a short particle burst; effects clean themselves up. Hits now notify an optional `IMagicHitReceiver`, allowing the courtyard slime to take damage. Prefabs are in `Prefabs/MagicBolt.prefab` and `Prefabs/MagicImpact.prefab`. Use **MVP03 > Add or Rebuild Magic Attack** to update the assets and attach the caster to the open courtyard; save the scene afterwards. **MVP03 > Capture Magic Preview** captures the current camera to `Previews/MVP03_Magic.png` (pause during a shot to capture the effect).

## Camera pitch slider

The upper-right **相机俯角** panel adjusts the downward angle from **5 to 45 degrees** in one-degree steps while the game runs. Click the track or drag its handle; **重置 18°** restores the scene's default angle. Turning the view keeps the character's torso centred and preserves the current zoom distance and horizontal heading. Q/E and the mouse wheel continue to zoom.

Player, companion and slime sprites match the camera's full orientation, compensating for its downward pitch so their pixel proportions stay intact at higher angles. Only the visual rotates about its foot pivot; movement roots and collision capsules stay upright. Four-direction atlas orientation and slime squash/stretch are preserved. The shared character shader still casts upright silhouettes toward each light, with CPU bounds enclosing both the tilted visual and these silhouettes. `Previews/MVP03_BillboardPitch.png` and `Previews/MVP03_BillboardPitchValidation.json` record the result and checks at 5, 18 and 45 degrees.

`CameraPitchUI` uses a screen-overlay Canvas and the Input System UI module. Its pointer-only EventSystem leaves WASD, arrows and Space with the character. The panel stays clear of the scene's pixel/depth-of-field passes. The selected angle is saved locally in PlayerPrefs under `MVP03.CameraPitch` after a short debounce, on focus loss and when the UI closes; it returns in the next Play session or launch. Rebuilding the courtyard attaches the UI automatically. `Previews/MVP03_CameraPitchUI.png` shows it in Play, and `Previews/MVP03_CameraPitchUIValidation.json` records pointer interaction, limits, reset, preference reload, centred framing and unchanged zoom/native keyboard state.

## Click-to-place wet ground

**TA 制作与验收标准：** [潮湿地面与接触涟漪](../MVP04/ART_STANDARD.md#wet-ground-ta) 固化视觉目标、贴图与 Shader 契约、无数量上限的异步放置、涟漪预算、参数快照和验证要求。

**Left-click the courtyard paving or a stair tread to add a water stain.** Right-side UI, walls, character colliders and clicks outside the Game viewport are excluded. **清空地面** in the camera panel removes them, including queued placements. Placements last for the Play session with no configured count or queue-length limit; adding a stain never evicts an older one. Each placement rotates and scales the grayscale `Textures/GroundWaterStain.png` coverage mask, with a floor-support mask clipping it at tread/platform boundaries. The image was created using the built-in image generator; original pixels and the final prompt are preserved in `WaterStainArt.txt`. Import it as linear, uncompressed data; CPU Read/Write is no longer needed. Water has no collider and does not alter movement or combat.

**Placement is asynchronous.** A click performs one pick ray and enqueues a small stamp record. Reusable Unity Job buffers generate and batch the 48 × 48 support rays, then evaluate hits on workers. The main thread checks `IsCompleted` before completing a job and uploads only the 2.25 KB support mask. It never waits for an unfinished placement job. A FIFO retains every accepted placement and schedules one support job at a time; bursts drain across frames. Scheduling and `PendingCount` do not scan completed stamps. `PendingCount` / `IsBusy` expose progress. Clearing invalidates the current generation so stale results cannot restore cleared water; disabling schedules buffer disposal after the outstanding job rather than blocking for it. Retained support textures and atlas rebuild work grow with the placement count; use Clear to release them.

**Overlapping stains merge on the GPU before shading.** `Resources/PuddleCoverage.shader` draws supported stain rectangles with `BlendOp Max` into one linear R8 atlas per water height (ARGB32 fallback). Repeating the same stamp cannot accumulate opacity, highlights or reflection. Atlas bounds are fixed to the configured ground at startup, with up to 64 texels/metre capped at 2048 pixels per axis; expanding a pool does not allocate or upload a full CPU atlas. Only affected heights rebuild, at most one per frame. The first atlas and mask shader are prepared on entering Play; one unused atlas is retained for reuse. Surface geometry and reflection culling stay bounded to the actual stamps. A soft threshold separates damp stone from the reflective water core. Texture changes queue rebuilds; ordinary parameter changes do not.

The reflection is an actual mirrored-camera render with an oblique clipping plane. It includes architecture, trees, lit characters and visible transparent magic, including objects outside the main view. HD2D portraits face the mirrored view only during capture, then their poses and bounds are restored. This prevents sprites from becoming edge-on in the reflection at high camera pitch. **The first version's art-directed mirror look is restored**, now shaped by the stain image: reflection weight `lerp(strength, 0.92, (1 - NdotV)^3)` multiplied by water coverage, a clear mip-0.6 reflection and subtle surface distortion. This deliberate 48% starting reflectance is a visual preset, not physical water F0. Strength zero disables reflection and sun highlights. Premultiplied transmission keeps the underlying stone visible. The water receives the scene's final volume and pixel passes.

All pools at the same height share one HDR reflection texture, capped at **768 pixels wide** and half the Game width. The dedicated `Rendering/PuddleReflectionRenderer.asset` skips SSAO, volume integration and post processing. A stationary camera refreshes at up to **60 Hz**; movement requests a fresh capture. At most **one plane is rendered per game frame**, with visible heights updated oldest-first. Actual capture frequency depends on game-frame timing; many different stair elevations share that budget and therefore refresh less often. Reflected interiors do not rerun volumetric scattering. The first reflection render and a zero-coverage water draw warm up on entering Play. One spare reflection target is retained across Clear; extra targets are released, and disabling releases all resources.

**Contacts disturb the water texture.** `PuddleRipples` detects CharacterControllers and colliders attached to a Rigidbody (including kinematic bodies). Entering/landing emits a displacement pulse; movement emits spaced pulses at intervals of at least 0.1 seconds. A stationary object does not continually emit. Static architecture and airborne objects do not disturb the surface. Compound rigidbodies emit once per water level. The actor footprint is multiplied by the actual water mask on the GPU, so touching the dry part of a stain produces no wave there.

`Resources/PuddleRipples.shader` integrates a damped two-dimensional wave equation in ping-pong RGHalf textures: current and previous height in metres. The fields share the coverage atlas's fixed world UVs; overlapping stamps therefore share one continuous wave field. Dry holes absorb waves, and different heights have independent fields. Height gradients perturb the real water normal, reflection UVs and lighting; no ring meshes or particles are spawned. The simulation runs at 30 fixed steps/second, interpolates between steps, uses at most 512 pixels per axis, and limits catch-up to two steps/frame. Wave speed is capped by the grid stability limit. At most four elevations retain active waves; the least recently disturbed is retired if the limit is reached. Idle fields expire after their damping tail and one buffer pair is kept warm. Clearing/removing pools or disabling the component releases their waves. There are no runtime GPU readbacks or CPU pixel loops. `Puddles.ContactRipples` profiles contact detection and GPU submissions.

Open **MVP03 > 水渍设置** for the live Editor panel: texture, water-core threshold, edge softness, wet darkening, reflection strength/blur, sun highlight, smoothness, wind ripple strength and tint. **接触涟漪** adds strength (0 disables simulation), propagation speed and damping. Defaults are strength 1.2, requested speed 1.1 m/s and damping 1.6/s. Changes save automatically, support Undo/Redo and survive leaving Play. **恢复第一版积水效果** restores reflection strength 0.48, darkening 0.32, blur 0.6, sun highlight 1, smoothness 0.97 and wind ripple strength 0.006; the stain threshold is 0.5 with 0.12 edge softness. Current user adjustments remain in `Materials/WetPuddle.mat`. Radius is on Main Camera's `ClickPuddles`; reflection resolution/rate are on `PuddleReflections`.

**MVP03 > Add Click-to-Place Puddles** installs the system without rebuilding the scene; future courtyard builds include it. `Previews/MVP03_AsyncWater.png` shows the restored effect with textured coverage. `Previews/MVP03_AsyncPuddleValidation.json` records GPU/CPU sampling alignment, exact duplicate invariance, offset overlaps, cancellation, stair support and live placement timings. Unity Profiler markers `Puddles.Enqueue`, `Puddles.ScheduleSupportJob`, `Puddles.FinishSupportJob` and `Puddles.GPUMaxUnion` separate the remaining main-thread work. The older water previews/reports document earlier presets and reflection/input/settings checks.

`Previews/MVP03_UnlimitedPuddleValidation.json` verifies a 100-click burst: all 100 requests remain queued, complete over 101 frames, retain the oldest stamp and merge into one surface. It also checks Clear while a job is active, new placements after Clear, and disabling/re-enabling with queued work. This supersedes the old report's 24-stamp eviction check. The separate contact-ripple queue limit remains unchanged.

`Previews/MVP03_ContactRipples.png` shows the contact response in the scene. `Previews/MVP03_ContactRippleValidation.json` records rigidbody/controller contact, movement and landing pulses, outward wave propagation, dry-island isolation, idle settling/reactivation, cleanup, matched renders with ripple normals enabled/disabled, and live Editor timings. The measured CPU marker includes contact queries and GPU submissions; it does not measure GPU execution time.

## Yellow ground fire and scorched stone

Select **黄色火焰** in the camera panel, then left-click supported paving or stair treads. **水塘** switches back to water. **清空地面** removes both elements, including queued placements. Placement is visual only: it does not damage actors or extinguish when water overlaps it.

`VFX/GroundFire.vfx` is an editable **Visual Effect Graph 17.3** asset. GPU particles sample the merged ground mask at birth, rise with buoyancy, and move through three-octave Perlin Curl Noise turbulence. Each height has one VFX instance, with an 8192-particle capacity and up to 12000 spawn attempts/second; dry births are rejected on the GPU. This simulation budget does not impose a placement-count limit. Different heights are separate systems, so their GPU cost adds up. Each distinct fire region retains a stable, shadowless, flickering yellow point light. Neighbouring clicks at the same elevation share a light within `min(2.5 m, lightRange * 0.4)`; adding distant fires or moving the camera never reassigns existing lights. Four lights are prewarmed; more are allocated as needed, and Clear releases extras.

Both `RebuiltRenderer` and `PuddleReflectionRenderer` use **Forward+**, so the combined courtyard mesh can receive all relevant fire lights without the Forward per-object light limit. Stone and character shaders already implement the clustered light loop. Lights remain subject to URP platform limits and GPU cost; this does not promise unlimited visible lights at a fixed frame rate. `Previews/MVP03_MultiFireLightingValidation.json` verifies 12 separated fires, stable light identities after adding fires and moving the camera, actual light contribution at every position on one combined stone mesh through both renderers, and cleanup.

The authored **4 × 4 / 16-frame** `Textures/FlameFlipbook.png` animates with random starting phases and adjacent-frame blending. Its neutral emission and original alpha are preserved; the graph adds yellow-to-orange colour. Height now controls both particle lifetime and visual tongue height, with a **0.55** preset and **0.44** width. The source prompt is recorded in `FireArt.txt`. The existing scene pixel pass also applies to the flame.

An irregular **scorched-ground layer** uses the same support-clipped Max-union footprint. Static charcoal grain darkens the existing stone without replacing its seams. Sparse dark-red embers flicker independently. Repeating a stamp does not repeatedly darken the ground or stack separate particle systems. Water and fire retain independent coverage, and water reflection/ripple budgets are unchanged. Placement uses the existing asynchronous FIFO and never waits on unfinished support jobs.

Open **MVP03 > 地面火焰设置** to adjust height, width, particle Pivot XYZ, density, rise speed, animation frame rate, turbulence intensity/frequency, brightness, scorch darkness/grain scale, embers and local lights. Pivot uses local particle-size units: zero is the centre and Y = -0.5 is the bottom edge; the default is (0, -0.42, 0). It is exposed as `ParticlePivot` in the graph, driven by the material in the scene, and updates both new and existing particles. Changes preview live, support Undo/Redo, and save across Play. **打开 Visual Effect Graph** opens the graph. **MVP03 > Add Ground Fire** installs its scene references; **Rebuild Ground Fire VFX Graph** regenerates the graph and replaces manual graph edits.

See `Previews/MVP03_GroundFire.png` for the actual Game output and `Previews/MVP03_GroundFireValidation.json` for overlap invariance, 64 mixed queued placements, one VFX per height, UI rejection, cancellation and resource cleanup. This is a GPU particle flame with an animated texture, not a fluid solver. Arbitrary placement counts are not a guaranteed frame-rate target.

## Blue slime enemy

Click **+ 生成敌人 · 蓝色史莱姆** below the camera slider to create one enemy near the player. The spawner searches visible positions 2.6–5.4 metres away, checking capsule clearance, floor support and an unobstructed path from the player. If there is no suitable space, the panel asks you to move and retry. Each new slime inherits the existing lit sprite, hopping, damage and respawn behaviour; its spawn point becomes its respawn home. Added enemies only last for the current Play session. Rebuilding the slime configures the camera's `CourtyardEnemySpawner` automatically. `Previews/MVP03_EnemySpawnUI.png` shows the panel, and `Previews/MVP03_EnemySpawnValidation.json` records three consecutive UI clicks, placement checks and unchanged camera/keyboard state.

A blue slime starts at `(-0.2, 0.09, -15.2)` in front of the player's initial courtyard position. It performs short ballistic hops with anticipation, stretch in the air and a compressed landing. Within 8.5 metres it hops toward the player, stopping its approach at about 1.15 metres. Its single front sprite always faces the camera, including pitch compensation; pursuit and recoil never turn or mirror it. Front alignment is applied on configuration/startup and refreshed after movement, with both sprite flip flags locked off. The actual `CharacterController` uses a 1.24-metre-wide, 1.35-metre-tall capsule (radius 0.62, centre height 0.685), with its base kept at the original foot level. It moves up and down, collides with the world and limits the path; this is a simple direct pursuit prototype, without pathfinding or player contact damage.

**Two Space-key bolt hits defeat it.** Hits briefly brighten the lit sprite and push it back; defeat flattens the body before hiding it. It respawns after four seconds at its original position when the player has moved clear. Select the **BlueSlime** scene object to tune hop height/speed, waiting time, detection range, hit points and respawn delay. **MVP03 > Add or Rebuild Blue Slime** updates `Prefabs/BlueSlime.prefab` and adds one if the scene has none. Courtyard rebuilds include the enemy.

The sprite uses the same **MVP04/Lit Pixel Character** shader as the hero, with rounded normals, received light/shadows and a light-facing shadow silhouette. The existing camera's subtle pixel pass and depth of field also apply. There is no extra light on the enemy. `Textures/BlueSlime.png` was created with the built-in image generator using the hero atlas as a style reference; its original alpha is preserved, and the importer slices the occupied bounds and places the pivot at the base. The full prompt and provenance are in `BlueSlimeArt.txt`. `Previews/MVP03_BlueSlime.png` and `Previews/MVP03_BlueSlimeValidation.json` contain the scene preview and live behaviour checks.

The courtyard is rebuilt around the supplied reference: close stone piers and a staircase frame a compact playable route. Beveled blocks, irregular pavers and carved arches use a weathered limestone albedo with normal relief. The `World Stone PBR` shader projects that material in world space so it retains the same scale on walls, stairs and pillars. Geometry is combined into saved mesh assets for a small number of renderers.

The tree uses narrow leaf meshes and tapered branches, with real leaf shadows across the masonry. Warm sunlight, cooler ambient fill, SSAO, ACES grading and bokeh depth of field establish the light and depth. MVP03 now has its own renderer; its index is added to the existing PC pipeline without changing that pipeline's default renderer.

## Warm volumetric sunlight (MVP04 migration)

MVP03 shares MVP04's `WindowVolumeFeature`, single-scattering ray-march shader, cached 3D noise, moving air, edge-aware denoising and depth-aware upsampling. Its open courtyard uses the **main directional sun** as its volumetric source. Golden light arrives along `(0.18, -0.64, -0.746)` (normalized), passing through the actual tree canopy and architecture. The volume samples the same cascaded shadow atlas as the scene, using hardware comparison filtering and subsequent volume denoising. Shadow strength is bound per camera. Sunlight is parallel; it has no inverse-square falloff. Both incoming and camera paths use Beer-Lambert attenuation and the same density field.

The medium occupies `(-13.9, 0, -30.4)` to `(12, 15, 3.4)`. Unity's old linear fog is disabled. Opaque mortar cores behind the separated stone blocks close light leaks through solid wall joints. The existing geometry, sprites, magic and mild pixel finish are retained. Focus still tracks the character, with a 70 mm / f4 lens setting to keep the close zoom usable.

| Parameter | Preset |
| --- | --- |
| Sun RGB / intensity | `(1, 0.90, 0.72)` / `3.4` |
| Extinction / scattering albedo / HG g | `0.022 m^-1` / `0.92` / `0.35` |
| Density variation / frequency | `0.7` / `0.38` |
| Air speed / warp / fine layer | `0.18 m/s` / `1.1 m` / `0.35` |
| Volume resolution / steps | One third width and height / 32 view steps / 1 light-path sample |
| Denoising | `0.9` |

Open **MVP03 > 体积光设置** for the dedicated Editor window. It provides sun direction, colour, intensity and shadow strength; volume on/off, quality and denoising; extinction, scattering, density variation, and air speed/direction/warp/detail. Changes preview immediately, support Undo/Redo, and save automatically after a short delay. **Adjustments made in Play survive returning to Edit mode.** The performance preset retains one-third resolution with 32 view steps; standard uses half resolution / 40 steps, and fine uses full resolution / 64 steps.

Sun settings live in `Rendering/CourtyardSun.asset`. The scene's `CourtyardSun` component applies this profile to the directional light in Edit and Play: use the window or profile Inspector to tune it. Character, environment and volume all receive this same light. The medium remains in `Materials/WarmAirVolume.mat`, also available through **MVP03 > Select Warm Volume Settings**. Renderer quality and bounds live on **Warm courtyard / MVP04 single scattering** in `Rendering/RebuiltRenderer.asset`.

**MVP03 > Apply Warm Volumetric Light and Camera** upgrades the open scene without rebuilding geometry. It applies the frontal camera and focus preset, preserves existing sun-profile/medium/quality values, and does not duplicate wall cores or renderer features. **MVP03 > Apply Front Camera and Lit Characters** applies just the camera, character materials and sun-profile binding. Future reference-courtyard builds include these changes. MVP04 keeps its own medium material, bounds and three window sources; directional scattering is opt-in and remains disabled there.

`Previews/MVP03_WarmVolumeGame.png` shows actual Play output. `Previews/MVP03_WarmVolumeValidation.json` records linear-HDR checks for warm scattering, geometric occlusion, source-off extinction, finite pixels, unchanged MVP04 source selection/bounds, character follow, zoom limits and live Editor frame timings. This is real-time single scattering with finite shadow-map and ray-step resolution.

## Pixel finish and assets

A subtle full-screen pixel pass runs after post processing, covering the scene and transparent magic together. It uses **2x2 screen-pixel cells at 55% blend** with the original image, retaining fine detail, bloom gradients and the existing colour palette. Adjust **Pixel Size** and **Pixel Blend** on `Materials/SubtlePixels.mat`; blend 0 disables the visual effect, while 0.35–0.65 is the intended mild range. The effect belongs to the dedicated MVP03 renderer. **MVP03 > Apply Subtle Pixel Post Process** installs it without rebuilding the scene and preserves existing material settings. **MVP03 > Capture Subtle Pixel Comparison** captures the same frame with and without the effect to `Previews/MVP03_SubtlePixels.png` and `Previews/MVP03_SubtlePixels_Original.png` (pause during a spell to include the magic).

The playable character now uses MVP04's **PilgrimWalk** atlas and animation set directly: front, left, right and back each have six separate frames (24 total). W shows the back, S the front, A the left and D the right. `PixelWalkAnimation` advances from actual movement distance, keeping steps tied to travel speed; stopping or pushing into a wall returns to frame 0 while retaining the facing direction. Point filtering, uncompressed pixels, shared foot pivots and the existing MVP04 sprite scale are retained. Use **MVP03 > Use MVP04 Animated Character** to reapply this character without changing the camera or lights. Scene rebuilds include it automatically.

The stationary companion retains its original 56-pixel-high sprite. Both characters use MVP04's **Lit Pixel Character** shader through independent MVP03 materials (`PilgrimWalk_Lit` and `RebuiltTraveler1_Lit`). Their albedo responds to the main sun, environment light and additional lights, including the bolt's cyan point light. Rounded billboard normals and a restrained back-light response make side lighting readable. They receive realtime tree/architecture shadows, write alpha-tested depth and normals, and cast two-sided silhouettes oriented toward each light; `PixelCharacterShadow` updates the bounds as animation frames change. `PixelFocus` follows the playable character's depth so the sprite remains in focus while moving; foreground stairs and distant architecture soften. The original courtyard source art and prompts are recorded in `ArtGenerationPrompts.txt`; the shared walk atlas is in `Assets/MVP04/Textures/PilgrimWalk.png`.

`Previews/MVP03_FrontLitCharacters.png` shows the frontal camera and character lighting in Play. `Previews/MVP03_LitCharacterValidation.json` records isolated linear-HDR tests for coloured lights, intensity response, cookies, main sunlight and received shadows. `Previews/MVP03_PresentationValidation.json` records camera alignment, both character assignments, window callbacks, Undo/Redo and Play-to-Edit persistence.

`Previews/MVP03_AnimatedTraveler.png` shows the updated playable character. `Previews/MVP03_AnimatedTravelerValidation.json` checks all 24 frames through the live movement controller, four-way facing, idle/blocked behaviour, screen-axis orientation and the lit material assignment.

Use **MVP03 > Build HD2D Church** or **MVP03 > Rebuild Reference Courtyard** to regenerate the scene and assets. **MVP03 > Capture HD2D Preview** writes `Previews/MVP03_Chapel.png`. **MVP03 > Archive > Build Earlier Courtyard** writes the previous courtyard to `Scenes/MVP03_PreviousCourtyard.unity`. The original uniformly pixelated chapel generator remains under **MVP03 > Build Pixel Chapel Archive**.
